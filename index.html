You own the record. You still pay to hear it.

Stack makes owning the record the thing that unlocks it everywhere.

Your collectionAdd records by barcode, Discogs or the Puck. Browse covers, a crate or a list.

    The risks, and the answers

    Tap a card to flip it.

    RiskMusic rights

    Owning a record doesn't give you the right to stream it, or to copy it.

    Tap for the answer
    AnswerPartner with labels

    Many records already come with a download code. Stack becomes the home for it, licensed, with royalties on every play.

    RiskHardware costs

    Making, certifying and shipping the Puck is expensive.

    Tap for the answer
    AnswerStart small, keep it optional

    Barcode and Discogs import work without it. The Puck launches in small batches through record shops.

    RiskAn empty marketplace

    Buyers won't come without sellers, and sellers won't come without buyers.

    Tap for the answer
    AnswerLaunch city by city

    Start in Manchester with partner record shops listing their second-hand stock from day one.

    (1) The problem

    You own the record.
    You still pay to hear it.

    Records and phones don't talk to each other. Own The Dark Side of the Moon on vinyl, and you still pay a streaming service to hear it on the bus.

    (2) The solution

    Owning the record
    unlocks it everywhere.

    Add a record once by barcode, Discogs or the Puck, and it plays on every device, for your whole family.

    (3) The Puck

    A spin becomes
    your own copy.

    The Puck sits between turntable and speakers. Sound passes straight through while it records your copy in hi-res and logs every play.

    (4) The market

    £0mUK vinyl sales in 2025.

    Up 0% in a year, and the highest in around 30 years. Collectors want to own music again.
